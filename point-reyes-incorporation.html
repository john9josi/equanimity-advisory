Point Reyes Incorporation Options

Point Reyes Station is not a city. It is an unincorporated village. Marin County is the local government.

The Village Association can stay a civic forum. It can also ask the County for a firmer seat, form a special district, or try to become a city.

Each option gives a different power. Each option also takes more time, money, and volunteer work.

This is a briefing for PRSVA. It is not legal advice and not a recommendation. Marin LAFCO, the Board of Supervisors, the Coastal Commission, and local voters decide any formal change.

How it works now

Read the stack from the top. The County makes the decisions. PRSVA writes comments. Districts run some services.

Today’s government stack

Neighbors

First, name the problem

Two different problems. Two different tools.
What is the village trying to fix?

Land use

Tourism, design review, housing, the historic downtown, dark skies, parking, and the Community Plan.

A CSD cannot take these powers. Only a city gets zoning. The Coastal Act still applies after that.

If cityhood is too heavy: update the Community Plan, or ask the County for a municipal advisory council.

Services

Wastewater, visitor bathrooms, parks, local roads, streetlights, or a community hall.

A special district can run these. California law treats a CSD as an alternative to a new city, or as a step toward one.

West Marin pattern: Tomales and Muir Beach formed CSDs. They still do not control zoning.

        If PRSVA cares most about…

        Village character and the Community Plan

        Do not expect a CSD to become the planning department. Update the Community Plan. Keep design review sharp. Ask the County if a municipal advisory council would give a harder seat. Cityhood is the only structural upgrade for zoning. Price that with a fiscal study first.

        Wastewater, bathrooms, or a village hall

        Study a CSD or a county service area for a short list of services. Tomales CSD is the closest local model for sewer and community facilities. Water already has a district: North Marin.

        A louder voice in San Rafael, without a new government

        A municipal advisory council is the tool for this. The Supervisors make it, name its duties, and can fund it. PRSVA must decide if it becomes that council or sits beside it.

        Grants, insurance, and a way to hire help

        Incorporate PRSVA as a California nonprofit. A 501(c)(3) fits education and grants. A 501(c)(4) fits more direct lobbying. Neither one changes who issues permits.

        What cityhood requires

        1. 01

          Draw a map

          The area must have at least 500 registered voters. Point Reyes Station is near that line. A tight village map may fail the count. A wide map through ranchland is a harder political fight.

        2. 02

          File a petition or a resolution

          A voter petition needs about 25 percent of registered voters in the proposed city. An affected agency can also adopt a resolution. File a notice of intent before you collect signatures.

        3. 03

          Pay for a fiscal analysis

          Marin LAFCO must find that the city can pay for services and a reserve for three years. This study is the real gate. Plan for years of work and a six-figure cost before anyone is mayor.

        4. 04

          Make the County roughly whole

          Revenue neutrality means the County should not lose the tax base and keep the service costs. The new city often pays for that gap. Sales tax and hotel tax do not all stay in the village by default.

        5. 05

          Win LAFCO, then win an election

          CEQA review and a sphere-of-influence finding come with the case. Voters in the area then vote on incorporation. New California cities almost always start as general-law cities.

        6. 06

          Keep the agencies that already work

          Incorporation does not take the National Seashore, cancel Coastal Commission review, or move water off NMWD. Police would likely be a sheriff contract. Fire would likely stay County Fire.
